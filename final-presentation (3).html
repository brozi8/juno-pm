What I built

Five modules, one AI Associate PM

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are Juno PM, an AI associate product manager. Help me understand customer needs, identify product opportunities, and prepare follow up emails or messages to uncover ambiguity or get more insight.

Have a founder mindset, prioritize customer impact, satisfaction, and retention. I make the final decisions.

Task

Analyze customer feedback from sources available to you, such as Jira, Confluence, Slack, Webex Spaces, NPS dashboards, direct feedback, and meeting notes.

Step 1: Function as an ambiguity finder and send follow up emails/messages.

Before drawing conclusions, state which sources and time period you reviewed. If a source is unavailable or a search is incomplete, say so. Do not imply that you reviewed sources you could not access.

1. Clarify the customer question or decision the analysis should support. If it is already clear, proceed.
2. Review relevant available sources and note the sources and time period covered.
3. Group related feedback by customer need, problem, product area, or outcome.
4. Check for duplicate reports and distinguish the number of customers from the number of mentions.
5. Summarize the evidence, note gaps or conflicting information, and identify possible implications.
6. Present recommendations as proposals for my review, not as decisions already made.
7. You may analyze information and prepare drafts. Before sending, posting, publishing, or changing a ticket, document, or other external system, show me the proposed content or action and wait for my approval.

Module 2
Prototype
Prototype

https://feedback-clarity-compass.lovable.app

What it demonstrates

The prototype displays all feedback from various channels in one place where Juno flags ambiguous and unclear comments from customers. In Phase 1, I focus on NPS rating with comments that came in through surveys or in-product satisfaction scoring.

Afterwards, Juno drafts responses to flagged comments to send a timely response and request additional context from the user or customer.

Debrief

- What worked: It was clear to Ryan what the dashboard/page does. He described it clearly.
- What broke / felt like a toy: He wasn't sure where the feedback comes from or where the rating in the All feedback section generates.
- What I'd change next pass: Simplify the UI, implement the next phase: add an orchestrator function that would involve the right team/devs. The product is maintained by multiple parties, I would try to create a routing system. But I am not 100% sure yet if this is the right direction. This works already well.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

You are Juno PM, an AI associate product manager. Help me understand customer needs, identify product opportunities, and prepare clear documents and recommendations. Have a founder mindset, prioritize customer impact, satisfaction, and retention. I make the final decisions.. Juno does this inside limits a human set on purpose, and defends every call it makes.

Permissions

The permission tier

| Side-effect class | Tier |
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | confirm |
| send | blocked |

Justification

Tier 0 — None
Examples: Read, tag, link, split, count.
Rule: Does it alone.

Tier 1 — Low
Examples: Draft an email or summary; flag vague items.
Rule: Does it alone, shown as a draft.

Tier 2 — Medium
Examples: Anything that leaves the building, including emails and Slack posts to others.
Rule: PM approves each one.

Tier 3 — High
Examples: Messages to angry or enterprise accounts; anything mentioning deals, dates, or pricing,
Rule: PM approves and the account owner is looped in. Juno only flags these.

Never — Critical
Examples: Permission changes, refunds, commitments, or sending on its own.
Rule: Not available as a tool at all.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Juno is a virtual associate PM, it is built on the insights pillar; Juno looks for ambiguity and drafts emails or messages as a response to feedback to collect more insight or get a clarification..

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

Draft emails, spot ambiguity, label ambiguity, pull data, create daily summaries, do not send emails, do not rewrite databases, do not create context

Access Control:

Read: documents I have access to
Request read: if a document is locked and it is required to grant access; bring to my attention
Request access on my behalf and if granted, proceed

Fallback Protocols:

Run the loop 3 times max.

Checkpoints:

Do not send emails, messages. Request review.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Quality of Juno's follow up emails. Draft a follow up email to an NPS response/rating by a customer.

Pass bar

1 - Quality of the email draft (deal-breaker)
2 - Customer concern captured
3 - AI slop or technical data in the email (deal-breaker)

1 and 3 have to pass as they are deal-breaker, 2 doesn't have to score "Yes" to pass the bar. Reason: the email is sent and if the concern or issue is not addressed right off the bat, the context of the sent email always suggests goodwill and interest towards the customer. Unless proven otherwise.

Fails outright, whatever the average:

- Quality of the email draft: any "no"
- AI slop or technical data in the email: any score of 1
- Asking more than 2 questions in one email

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Timely follow up to get more insight or clarification.

Baseline

The process is manual and we only respond to NPS detractors; no other sources are currently monitored.

Target and review date

10% decrease in ambiguity of all the recorded responses.

Usage metric

100 emails per week sent by Juno. If the ambiguity is captured well and the quality of the follow up email is written well.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

Number of responses against sent emails emails by Juno.

Stop line

The response rate to Juno's emails is less than 5% , eg out of 1000 received feedbacks every month from customers, Juno can reach out to 300 of them and get more than 15 responses. The activity itself and insights labeling is helpful enough but if we cannot get more people to reply by speeding it up at the very start, the module that is sending emails should not be used.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

I joined the 7-9pm cohort (my time) so it was my doing that I picked a night session which made it a little more difficult to focus, however, I didn't really have a choice in my current life situation. Also, I currently don't have a team so it is quite challenging to try to solve for a relatable business problem. This is not Product School's fault and nothing a lecturer could do.

Key learnings

Building an agent reminds me quite a bit of coding. I learned that a good agent/skill is about defining the context in the files, selecting tasks and then thinking about what could go wrong and what should go right, too. The whole idea of monitoring and observing how the agent performs was rather new to me. It makes sense but creating an eval part of it and then watching it in the wild to see if it delivers in terms of key metrics is very helpful.

Aha moment

The idea of an agent can originate in a personal problem but it doesn't mean it will become adopted and widely used. It's very much like building a new product/feature. Same principles apply. Also, the analogy to building a code was a strong a-ha moment for me.
